> _"I never really planned to become a photographer. I just kept making things. And somewhere along the way, I became one."_

---

## 1. The Name Before Photography

Loyal Manuka began as a YouTube/channel-name idea during my early **BGMI (Battlegrounds Mobile India)** phase after I got my mobile. I wanted to create a gaming channel and needed a name that stood out.

I searched for names using _"Loyal"_ and found hundreds of channels already using it. I then thought about _"Manuka"_; there were a few results, but not much. I decided to merge the two words, searched **"Loyal Manuka,"** and found no one using it in the way I wanted.

I started using **Loyal Manuka** for YouTube, games, usernames, and IDs. At that point, it was simply a username—not a photography brand, not a portfolio, just an identity for a gamer.

---

## 2. YouTube and the First Content

I created the Loyal Manuka YouTube channel and posted my very first BGMI montage. It did not perform particularly well.

Around half a year later, I created the **@loyalmanuka** Instagram account. The original plan was still short-form gaming clips and BGMI edits, but gradually, the page transformed into a sandbox where I began experimenting with whatever caught my curiosity.

---

## 3. The Experimental Instagram Era

During this period, I posted viral-style reels using trending audio, kinetic text, and fast effects, alongside many other visual experiments:

- College campus moments
- Tech events & college fests
- Electronics and circuit projects
- Car aesthetics & CapCut speed ramps
- Lyrical reels & mood edits

I was not yet trying to build a photography identity. I was trying to understand **how to create**, how pacing worked, and what stories people responded to on screen.

---

## 4. College Became Content

I started treating my college environment as an everyday film set.

For one early reel, I bunked a lecture, walked around the college corridors and courtyard, shot spontaneous footage, and edited it into a fast reel. It unexpectedly reached **5.3K views**.

Later, I attended **VSIT Tech Vista**, documented the vibe, and made another reel that crossed **1K views**. Creating was becoming a daily reflex.

---

## 5. Electronics and StudKits

Around the same time, I was also building electronics hardware. I created content around technical projects—including an IoT intruder-alert system—and shared process breakdowns.

Some of these were collaborations with **StudKits**, partly aimed at helping grow that community. During this era, Loyal Manuka was an authentic mix: technology, student life, gaming, cars, edits, and early fragments of Mumbai.

---

## 6. Car Edits and CapCut

Cars were another obsession. I experimented with automotive video aesthetics. For a BMW edit, I curated different angles downloaded from Pinterest, sliced them with transitions and beat-drops in CapCut, and paired them with Shubh's hit track _"You and Me"_.

These edits taught me rhythm, color grading, and visual tempo—skills that later became foundational when transitioning to still photography.

---

## 7. Discovering Photography & The First Edits

Long before I called myself a photographer, I had already been quietly taking photos of scenes I found compelling—often keeping them in my camera roll without ever posting.

Then I downloaded **Adobe Lightroom Mobile**. I began moving sliders based purely on what looked visually dramatic. My early edits were high in contrast, heavy on saturation, and bold.

I began closely studying other photographers, analyzing their framing, perspective, and color palettes to understand why certain frames resonated so deeply. One of my biggest inspirations was **[@haramkhor](https://instagram.com/haramkhor)**—whose distinctive visual voice across Mumbai I still admire and hope to meet in person one day.

---

## 8. The 2024 Wrapped Turning Point

![2024 Wrapped Photo Series](</images/timeline/2024%20wrap/wrap%202024(1).jpg>)

At the end of the year, I curated and posted my **2024 Wrapped** photo carousel.

This became my **first official photography series** in the true sense. Even though I was still finding my voice in Lightroom and street composition, the positive reception and engagement it received ignited a spark: _photography wasn't just a hobby on the side anymore—it was something I wanted to pursue seriously._

---

## 9. Monthly Photography Experiments

I committed to documenting my life month by month through photography dumps—January, February, March, and April.

Maintaining this rhythm forced me to stay observant every single day. My editing matured: harsh contrast gave way to intentional tones, natural shadows, and rich street palettes. The instinct to spot an unseen angle had always been there; now, the technical execution was catching up.

---

## 10. SOBO in Ganesh Chaturthi

![SOBO in Ganesh Chaturthi](/images/timeline/ganapati/ganapati%201.webp)

During Mumbai's most vibrant festival, I ventured into South Bombay to shoot **"SOBO in Ganesh Chaturthi."**

Within 3 to 4 days, the post crossed **1,000 views**. Seeing people connect with the emotion and devotion of South Mumbai gave birth to an ambitious new thought:

> _"What if I built a comprehensive, serialized visual project documenting Mumbai across its entire span?"_

---

## 11. The Idea of "SOBO to NOBO"

Mumbai is a city of sharp contrasts—from the colonial stone architecture and sea breeze of South Bombay (SOBO) to the kinetic, bustling neighborhoods of North Bombay (NOBO).

I decided to create **SOBO to NOBO**—a dedicated photography series exploring the transition across the city. For Episode 1, my cousin sister agreed to model. At the time, she was just starting her own public Instagram account, so we utilized Instagram's Collab feature to publish the joint frame.

---

## 12. SOBO to NOBO EP1 — The First Major Milestone

![SOBO to NOBO EP1](/images/timeline/sobo-nobo%20ep1/sobo-nobo%201.jpg)

When Episode 1 dropped, the community response was unprecedented:

- **2,567+ Views**
- **660 Accounts Reached**
- **102 Likes** _(My first post to cross the 100-likes mark)_
- **25 Comments, 6 Shares, 4 Reposts, 3 Saves, 41 Profile Visits**

Crossing triple-digit likes for the first time was more than just a metric—it was proof of concept. **SOBO to NOBO** was no longer just an experiment; it was a defining photography project.

---

## 13. SOBO to NOBO EP2 & WallMag Award Rank #19

![SOBO to NOBO EP2](/images/timeline/sobo-nobo%20ep2/sobo-nobo%20ep2%201.webp)

Pushed by the momentum of EP1, I went deeper into the city streets to shoot **SOBO to NOBO Episode 2**.

The work spoke for itself: **SOBO to NOBO EP2 was awarded Top 100 Finalist at the WallMag Mobile Photography Awards 2026, securing Rank #19 nationally.**

Having mobile street work recognized alongside some of the best mobile photographers in the country cemented this chapter as a turning point in my creative journey.

---

## 14. "Inn Dino, Mumbai" — Fragments of a City in Passing

Rather than staging photographs, I became obsessed with collecting raw, candid fragments of daily life: commuters rushing at dawn, shadows slicing colonial facades, antique yellow taxis, and fleeting glances.

This body of work became:

> **"Inn Dino, Mumbai — Fragments of a city in passing."**

It was never designed to be a polished postcard of Mumbai. It was Mumbai experienced in real time—raw, kinetic, nostalgic, and alive.

---

## 15. WallMag Recognition for "Inn Dino" (Rank #70)

The _Inn Dino, Mumbai_ series earned its own national recognition: **Top 100 Finalist in the WallMag Travel Photography Awards 2026, achieving Rank #70.**

This marked my **second national WallMag Top 100 award in 2026**, validating both my street perspective (_SOBO to NOBO_) and travel documentation style (_Inn Dino_).

---

## 16. Chennai Photo Biennale Submission

Taking the work beyond social media and digital contests, I submitted _Inn Dino, Mumbai_ to the **Chennai Photo Biennale International Photography Open Call 2026–27** in the student photo series category.

Alongside the series, I also submitted my single-image study of a Royal Enfield Hunter parked in front of a Mumbai street mural.

---

## 17. The First Self-Pitched Brand Collaboration: Royal Enfield

While walking past an old Mumbai wall painted with vibrant street art, I framed a Royal Enfield Hunter against the textures of the stone.

Instead of simply posting the image and tagging the brand in the caption, I decided to pitch myself directly. I reached out to the **Royal Enfield First Mumbai Store**, introduced myself as Loyal Manuka, showed them the framing, and asked if they would be open to collaborating on the post.

To my surprise, they agreed immediately without asking for a pre-screening. Two hours after posting, they accepted the Instagram collaboration. The frame went live on their official store account, garnering over **1.3K views** on my feed. It was my very first independently pitched brand partnership.

---

## 18. Looking Back: The Evolution

Loyal Manuka was never an overnight master plan. It was forged through relentless trial, error, and curiosity:

```
BGMI Montages
  → Tech & College Reels (5.3K views)
    → CapCut Beat Syncing & Car Edits
      → First Lightroom Experiments & Studying Masters
        → 2024 Wrapped Photo Series
          → SOBO in Ganesh Chaturthi
            → SOBO to NOBO EP1 (First 100+ Likes)
              → SOBO to NOBO EP2 (WallMag Rank #19)
                → Inn Dino, Mumbai (WallMag Rank #70)
                  → Chennai Photo Biennale International Open Call
                    → Royal Enfield Brand Collaboration
```

Almost every single milestone started not with a rigid business strategy, but with three simple words: **"What if?"**

---

## The Part I Don't Want to Forget

The most important part of this journey has never been the metrics—not the view counts, not the likes, not the certificate ranks, and not the brand tags.

**It is that I kept making things.**

I kept testing ideas, learning from what flopped and what resonated, and following my curiosity wherever it wandered. Loyal Manuka began as a random username I came up with because I needed a name for a YouTube channel. Three years later, it represents an art form I deeply love and live by.

I never planned to become a photographer. I just kept creating. And somewhere along the way, I became one.
